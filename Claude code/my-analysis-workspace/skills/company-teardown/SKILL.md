# SKILL: Company Teardown

## What this skill does
Produces a consistent, structured strategic analysis of an AI biotech company.
Use it whenever I say "do a teardown of [company]" or "analyse [company]".

## When to use it
Any time I ask for an analysis, profile, or teardown of a company in the
AI drug discovery / TechBio space.

## Steps to follow
1. Search the web for current information on the company — funding, partnerships,
   what they actually do, recent news. Don't rely on memory; verify.
2. Produce the analysis in EXACTLY this structure:

### [Company Name]
**One-liner:** What they do in a single clear sentence.

**Business model:** One of — open-source / software platform / vertically
integrated (own wet lab or pipeline) / co-development with pharma. Explain
briefly how they make money.

**Pipeline position:** Where they sit in the drug development workflow
(Target ID → Hit Discovery → Lead Optimisation → Preclinical → Clinical →
Manufacturing → Enterprise). A company can span several.

**Protein class / modality** (if relevant): antibodies, enzymes, peptides,
small molecules, RNA/gene therapy, etc.

**Funding:** Total raised, most recent round, key investors. Cite the source.

**Pharma partnerships:** Named deals with terms where public. Cite each one.
If none are public, say so.

**Analyst's note:** ONE sharp sentence of strategic insight — their real bet,
their moat, or their key risk. This is the part a generic directory couldn't
write. Make it specific.

3. End with: any important caveats or things you couldn't verify.

## Rules
- Cite a source for every factual claim (funding, deals, trial stages).
- If you can't verify something, flag it — don't guess.
- Keep the analyst's note genuinely insightful, not generic.
- Be honest about weaknesses even for companies I like.
